## 0. The one-paragraph summary

Given a thermalized coarse ensemble at $(\beta_c, L_c)$, we want a thermalized
fine ensemble at $(\beta_f, L_f) = (4\beta_c, 2L_c)$ without paying for a cold
or hot HMC start at $\beta_f$, which is where critical slowing down and
topological freezing make direct simulation expensive or impossible. We do
this by pairing HMC-generated fine configurations with their exact coarse
partners (blocking is a fixed, deterministic, gauge-covariant map — no
learning happens here), training a score network to reverse a diffusion
process on the *fine* field conditioned on gauge-invariant coarse features,
and at generation time running that reverse diffusion with two additional
ingredients bolted on: (i) a soft guidance term that pulls the sampled fine
plaquettes toward consistency with the coarse plaquette they must sum to, and
(ii) a *hard*, structural correction that imposes the coarse ensemble's exact
topological charge on the fine sample by adding a fixed instanton field —
because topology is a discrete, non-local invariant of the wrapped
plaquette sum that the local diffusion dynamics cannot be trusted to get
right on its own. The output is a **starting configuration**, not a
certified sample from the fine Boltzmann distribution; a short classical HMC
tail (rethermalization sweeps) is what actually delivers exactness, and the
paper's exactness claim attaches to that tail, not to the network.

U(2) reuses this entire machinery unchanged, applied only to the U(1) phase
$\psi = \arg\det U$ that carries U(2)'s topology; the orthogonal SU(2)
degrees of freedom are never modelled — they are drawn from an *exact*
conditional heatbath sampler at fixed $\psi$, so nothing about the harder,
non-abelian sector is learned at all.

## 1. Naive blocking and the coarse/fine pairing

Blocking is the one deterministic, non-learned map in the whole pipeline. It
defines the training pairs and, transported through the character-expansion
identity, the topological charge.

**U(1).** A coarse link is the wrapped sum of the two fine links along the
same coarse-lattice edge:

$$\Theta_x(X,Y) = \mathrm{wrap}\big(\theta_x(2X,2Y) + \theta_x(2X{+}1,2Y)\big), \qquad
  \Theta_y(X,Y) = \mathrm{wrap}\big(\theta_y(2X,2Y) + \theta_y(2X,2Y{+}1)\big)$$

(`u1_2d/lgt/blocking.py`). This makes the coarse plaquette angle *exactly*
the wrapped sum of the four fine plaquette angles inside the corresponding
$2\times2$ cell — a telescoping identity, not an approximation — which is
what lets the topological charge be transported rather than re-derived at
every rung (§3). Tree level gives $\beta_c = \beta_f/4$; the exact
matched coupling instead solves a mean-plaquette condition against the
character expansion (`approx_matched_coarse_beta`).

**U(2).** Links are $U(2)$ matrices in the NTHMC split representation
$U = e^{i\phi}(q_0 I + i q_a\sigma_a)$. Blocking is the direct non-abelian
analogue — the *ordered product* of the two fine links, not a sum:

$$U_x^{\text{coarse}}(X,Y) = U_x(2X,2Y)\,U_x(2X{+}1,2Y), \qquad
  U_y^{\text{coarse}}(X,Y) = U_y(2X,2Y)\,U_y(2X,2Y{+}1)$$

(`u2_2d/lgt/blocking.py`). Because $\det$ is a homomorphism, the *determinant*
sector inherits the U(1) telescope verbatim: $\psi^{\text{coarse}} =
\mathrm{wrap}(\psi_1+\psi_2+\psi_3+\psi_4)$ over the four fine plaquettes,
even though the full matrix product does not commute. This is the single
fact that makes exact topology transport possible in a genuinely
non-abelian theory (§5). Same tree-level relation, $\beta_c=\beta_f/4$, in
every U(2) direction.

**What "naive" prolongation is, and where it is/isn't used.** For
*training*, no prolongation is needed at all: the network is trained on
pairs of an HMC-generated fine configuration and its exact coarse partner
computed by the map above (`RungData`, `u1_2d/model/train.py`). There is no
step where a coarse configuration is naively expanded into a fine one to
build training data.

At *generation* time, the U(1)/determinant sector's fine field is
initialized as uniform noise on the circle and denoised by the reverse SDE
(§4) — again, not by inverse-blocking the coarse field. Deterministic
inverse-blocking prolongators (`tile`: periodic replication; `halve`: the
exact algebraic inverse of the blocking sum, splitting each coarse link
evenly into its two fine constituents and zeroing the links blocking never
reads; `flux`: `halve` plus redistributing the coarse plaquette angle evenly
over the cell) exist only as **non-learned baselines** used to show the
network beats naive alternatives (`u1_2d/scripts/37_tiling_baseline.py`,
`66_prolongator_dispersion.py`, `67_prolongator_topology.py`; U(2)
equivalents `17_prolongator_baseline.py`, `20_prolongator_figure.py`,
`21_prolongator_observables.py`). They do **not** feed the deployed ladder.

The one place a naive inverse-blocking map genuinely is part of the deployed
pipeline is U(2)'s SU(2) sector (§5): each coarse SU(2) link is split into
two identical geodesic square-root halves and repeated to fill the
unconstrained fine links (`naive_su2_inverse_block`,
`u2_2d/model/su2_lift.py`). This naive seed then feeds an *exact* conditional
heatbath sampler, so — unlike a naive seed left to stand on its own — it
cannot bias the result; it only sets how many sweeps equilibration costs.

## 2. The diffusion process: SDE, schedule, sampler

The generative model is a **variance-exploding (VE) score-based diffusion**
on the circle — i.e. score-matching Langevin dynamics with a *wrapped*
Gaussian (heat-kernel-on-$S^1$) forward process, not a flat-$\mathbb{R}$
approximation, because link angles are periodic.

**Forward process.** $\theta_t = \mathrm{wrap}(\theta_0 + \sigma(t)\,z)$,
$z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, with $\mathrm{wrap}(\cdot)=\mathrm{atan2}(\sin\cdot,
\cos\cdot)$ applied after every operation. The exact score of this wrapped
kernel is a winding sum over image charges,
$\nabla\log p_\sigma(\delta) = \sum_k (\delta+2\pi k)\,
w_k(\delta,\sigma)/\sigma^2$ with softmax weights $w_k \propto
\exp(-(\delta+2\pi k)^2/2\sigma^2)$ (`u1_2d/model/wrapped.py`), and this is
what the network is trained to match — not a small-angle Gaussian
approximation.

**Noise schedule.** Geometric (log-linear) VE schedule,
$\sigma(t) = \sigma_{\min}(\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min})^t$, $t\in[0,1]$
(`u1_2d/model/schedule.py`). $\sigma_{\max}=6.0$ is chosen so the terminal
distribution is numerically uniform on the circle (the $q$-th Fourier mode
of the wrapped kernel is $e^{-q^2\sigma^2/2}\approx 10^{-8}$ at $\sigma=6$).
$\sigma_{\min}$ is coupling-aware: `sigma_min_beta_coef` sets an *effective*
floor $\sigma_{\min}(\beta)=\min(\sigma_{\min},\,c/\sqrt\beta)$, because
physical link fluctuations shrink as $1/\sqrt\beta$ and a fixed floor stops
resolving the target once $1/\sqrt\beta$ approaches $\sigma_{\min}$
(deployed coefficient: 0.30 for u1, 0.35 for u2).

**Reverse sampler.** Predictor–corrector, in the style of Song et al.:
- *Predictor* (ancestral/SMLD step): with
  $s=\sigma^2-\sigma_{\text{next}}^2$,
  $\theta \leftarrow \mathrm{wrap}\!\big(\theta + s\cdot\mathrm{score} +
  \sqrt{\max(0,\,s\,\sigma_{\text{next}}^2/\sigma^2)}\;z\big)$.
- *Corrector* (one Langevin MCMC step per predictor step, target
  signal-to-noise ratio 0.16): step size $\epsilon$ set from the ratio of the
  noise norm to the score norm, $\theta\leftarrow\mathrm{wrap}(\theta+
  \epsilon\,\mathrm{score}+\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,z)$.

**Step count.** $N=200$ reverse steps is the deployed/ladder-of-record
setting for both studies (`n_sampler_steps: 200` in every production
config). This is an accuracy choice, not a cost-minimal one: cutting to 25
steps gives a $\sim\!3\times$ speed-up at $\sim\!2.7\times$ the *raw-lift*
extended-loop error with no measurable change after the rethermalization
tail (§6) — so a deployment more sensitive to cost than to the raw-lift
number could reasonably run at 25; the paper reports the 200-step ladder
because that is the number the accuracy claims are measured against.

**Guidance terms added only at generation time** (not part of training),
composed additively onto the network's score:

1. **Blocking-consistency (reconstruction) guidance.** A soft penalty
   pulling each $2\times2$ cell's summed fine-plaquette angle toward the
   known coarse plaquette angle, $-\big(\sum_{\text{cell}}\phi_{\text{fine}}
   -\phi_{\text{coarse}}\big)^2 / 2\lambda(\sigma)$ with
   $\lambda(\sigma)=8\sigma^2+\epsilon$ so the constraint tightens as
   $\sigma\to0$; assembled through the same lattice-curl head the network
   itself uses (see §3), weight 1.0 by default. **This is the mechanism
   behind "sum of fine plaquettes = coarse plaquette"** — it is a gradient
   added during sampling, not a term in the training loss.
2. **Physics blend** (off by default; on in u1's `v3_scale.yaml`). Blends the
   learned score toward the *analytic* Wilson score of the noised target as
   $\sigma\to0$, weighted $w(\sigma)=1/(1+(\sigma/\sigma_c)^2)$. For U(2)'s
   determinant sector the analytic target is not Wilson-at-$\beta/4$ but the
   exact determinant-sector Bessel-ratio score (§5); this blend was tested
   for U(2) and found to actively corrupt topology at blend weight
   $\gtrsim0.5$, so it is deployed at 0.

## 3. The score network

**Inputs/outputs are gauge-invariant/covariant by construction, not by
loss.** The network never sees raw link angles. It consumes only
gauge-invariant channels of the *noisy* fine field — $\cos,\sin$ of the
plaquette angle and the $1\times2$/$2\times1$ rectangle angles (6 channels)
— concatenated with the same invariant channels of the coarse field,
upsampled to fine resolution as the conditioning signal (4–5 channels).

**Architecture** (`GaugeCovariantScoreNet`, fully convolutional, circular
padding throughout so it is lattice-size-agnostic):
- Stem: one $\mathrm{Conv2d}$ from $6+\text{cond}$ channels to `hidden`.
- Body: `depth` residual blocks, each two circularly-padded convolutions with
  **per-site channel normalization** (deliberately not batch/group norm,
  which would introduce a lattice-size dependence the fully convolutional
  design is meant to avoid) and **FiLM modulation** from a sinusoidal
  embedding of $(\sigma,\beta)$.
- Head: one $\mathrm{Conv2d}$ to a single scalar $h_p$ per plaquette
  (zero-initialized).
- **Curl reconstruction.** The scalar field $h_p$ is turned into a
  link-valued score via a discrete lattice curl,
  $s_x(x,y)=h(x,y)-h(x,y{-}1)$, $s_y(x,y)=h(x{-}1,y)-h(x,y)$ — which
  guarantees the network's output score is exactly gauge-covariant (orthogonal
  to gauge-orbit directions) by construction, and reproduces the exact form
  of the Wilson score, $h_p=-\beta\sin\theta_p$, through this same head.
- **Zero-initialized analytic residual.** A learned gate blends in the exact
  Wilson force $-(\beta\sigma)\sin\theta_p$ at the head, so the network only
  has to learn the *departure* from the known $\sigma\to0$ asymptote rather
  than the asymptote itself.

Deployed capacity: u2 uses `hidden=64, depth=4`; u1's campaign checkpoint
uses `hidden=56, depth=4` (a capacity-scaled variant, `v3_scale.yaml`, uses
`hidden=80, depth=5` and was found necessary once random-beta coverage was
widened — see §6).

**Training loss: denoising score matching, plus an optional soft
topological penalty.** For a noised sample $\theta_t=\mathrm{wrap}(\theta_0+
\sigma z)$ and $\delta=\mathrm{wrap}(\theta_t-\theta_0)$,

$$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DSM}} = \mathbb E\big\| \mathrm{model}(\theta_t,\sigma,
\beta,\mathrm{cond}) - \sigma\,\nabla\log p_\sigma(\delta) \big\|^2,$$

the exact wrapped-kernel score target from §2, not a flat-Gaussian
surrogate. An optional term penalizes the network's own *denoised* estimate
for drifting in a smooth surrogate for the topological charge,
$Q_{\mathrm{soft}}=\sum_p\sin\theta_p/2\pi$:
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{topo}} = \mathbb E\big[(Q_{\mathrm{soft}}(\hat\theta_0)
-Q_{\mathrm{soft}}(\theta_0))^2/(1+\sigma^2)\big]$, weighted
`topo_weight` (0.1 in both studies' campaign configs; note a later
CLAUDE.md finding that raising it to 0.3 does not move the sector match rate
and the recommendation was withdrawn — 0.1 is the number to quote). **This
is the only training-time physics term; the plaquette-consistency mechanism
described as item 1 in §2 is a sampling-time guidance term, not part of the
loss.**

## 4. Topological charge transport

The core design decision, and the reason the method's exactness claim can be
made at all: **topology is transported structurally, not left to the local
diffusion dynamics.** The wrapped plaquette sum that defines $Q$ is
invariant under any smooth deformation of the field until some plaquette
crosses $\pm\pi$ — a discrete, global event a local score network has no
mechanism to get exactly right, and empirically does not (raw match rate
degrades with volume; see `34_match_rate_volume` / `fig07`).

**Mechanism.** A fixed smooth instanton field of unit charge (a
$\theta_y(x,y)=2\pi x/L^2$ configuration with a transition-function
correction on the last column, so it is itself gauge-field-valid) is added,
scaled by the residual charge deficit between the coarse ensemble's known
$Q$ and the sample's current $Q$:

$$\theta \leftarrow \mathrm{wrap}\big(\theta + \Delta Q\cdot
  \mathrm{inst}\big), \qquad \Delta Q = Q_{\mathrm{coarse}} -
  \mathrm{round}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_p\theta_p\Big),$$

iterated up to 3 times to catch wrap-induced misses. This is applied *during*
the reverse SDE trajectory once $\sigma$ drops below a threshold (default
0.5, every 10 steps) — early enough that the sampler still has room to relax
the strain the correction introduces — and once more as an exact final pass.
Because blocking is an exact telescope (§1), $Q_{\mathrm{coarse}}$ *is*
$Q_{\mathrm{fine}}$ up to this transport, verified configuration-by-
configuration (not just on ensemble averages) in both studies.

**U(2)'s odd/even split.** $U(2)\cong(U(1)\times SU(2))/\mathbb Z_2$, so an
*even* change of $Q$ is a purely central move (free, $O(\beta/V)$, same
mechanism as above); an *odd* change necessarily also crosses the SU(2)
monodromy and needs a dedicated marginal move (`apply_winding`,
`set_topological_charge`) — no fixed shift field does the odd case cheaply.
Ladder transport therefore imposes even and odd charge changes by different
mechanisms, but the outcome is the same: $Q$ is *imposed*, never generated,
at every rung.

## 5. What is U(2)-specific: the split representation

U(2) reuses essentially all of §§1–4 unmodified, because of one structural
fact: $U=e^{i\phi}(q_0I+iq_a\sigma_a)$ factorizes the theory as
$p(\phi,q)=p(\psi)\,p(q\mid\psi)$ where $\psi=\mathrm{wrap}(2\phi)=\arg\det
U$ is a genuine compact U(1) field carrying *all* of the topology
($Q$ is a functional of $\psi$ alone), and — the key simplification — at
fixed $\phi$ the U(2) local weight is exactly $\exp(\beta\,k\cdot q)$, so
the **SU(2) sector has an exact conditional heatbath sampler and needs no
model at all**.

Consequently:
- **The score network is literally the U(1) network, reused, applied to
  $\psi$** (`det_lift.py` is a thin pass-through to the entire U(1) stack —
  same architecture, same DSM loss, same VE schedule, same PC sampler, same
  guidance terms). The only U(2)-specific input is the conditioning
  coupling: the net is conditioned on `matched_u1_beta` — the exact
  minimum-KL U(1) projection of the determinant-sector marginal weight
  $w_{\det}(\alpha) = 2I_1(z)/z$, $z=\beta\cos(\alpha/2)$ — never on the
  tree-level $\beta/4$, which differs from it by 23% at $\beta=4$.
- **The SU(2) sector is never learned.** At generation time each coarse
  SU(2) link is naively split (§1) to seed the fine field, then
  `conditional_su2_sweeps` — an exact heatbath sampler for $p(q\mid\psi)$ —
  equilibrates it at the diffused, fixed $\psi$ (20–30 sweeps). Because the
  sampler is exact, this step provably cannot introduce bias; it can only
  cost sweeps.
- **The joint density does not factorize** ($\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}\,P=
  \cos\omega_p\cos\phi_p$ is a product of cosines, not a sum of actions), so
  generating the two sectors *independently* and combining them would be
  wrong at $O(\phi^2\omega^2)$ — which is exactly why the SU(2) sampler must
  be conditional on the diffused $\psi$, not run beforehand or in parallel.

**Full U(2) ladder step, in order:** (1) diffuse $\psi_{\text{fine}}$ from
$\psi_{\text{coarse}}=\det$-phase of the coarse ensemble, with blocking
consistency + charge-transport guidance exactly as in §§2–4; (2) naive
inverse-block the coarse SU(2) part as a seed; (3) run 20–30 exact
conditional SU(2) heatbath sweeps at the now-fixed $\psi_{\text{fine}}$;
(4) a short joint rethermalization (§6) at the target coupling, where any
residual determinant-sector model error gets locally repaired.

## 6. What repairs the raw lift: rethermalization

The network's raw output is a **starting configuration, not a certified
sample.** Its density is measurably off the true Boltzmann distribution (KL
of several hundred to a few thousand nats/configuration, even where
four-significant-figure agreement holds on local observables — the
"dissociation" result) and its systematic bias is *flat* in loop size while
the theory's own per-configuration fluctuation grows by two orders of
magnitude, so large loops are where the raw lift is worst relative to what a
measurement needs.

The fix deployed at every rung is a short block of **exact classical local
sweeps** — heatbath (or Metropolis, for the Villain action) plus
overrelaxation, `topological_updates` switched **off** so the tail cannot be
credited with fixing topology it never touches; 10 sweeps for u1's ladder,
10 retherm sweeps (after 20–30 conditional SU(2) sweeps) for u2's. These
sweeps are demonstrably the right repair tool: matched in link-touches
against spending the same budget on further HMC trajectories, a handful of
sweeps reaches the exact plaquette to $|z|\le2$ at couplings where thousands
of trajectories of the trajectory-only arm never do, in both studies. Charge
transport (§4) is exact and untouched by this step; the sweeps repair local
structure only.

## 7. Training data ranges — u1 vs u2

Both studies condition the network on `model_beta` (u1: $\beta$ directly;
u2: `matched_u1_beta(beta_u2)`, the exact determinant-sector projection),
**not** on lattice size $L$ — the network is fully convolutional, so
coupling coverage, not size coverage, is what determines whether a rung is
in-distribution.

| | u1 (campaign, `v2.yaml`/`v3_scale.yaml`) | u2 (`default.yaml`/`v2.yaml`) |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed rungs | 4: $\beta=14.15,\,25.0,\,40.0,\,55.02$, all $L{=}16$ | 12, spanning $L=8,16,32,64$: $(\beta,L)=(3.5,8),(7.0,8),(14.0,8),(14.0,16),(28.0,16)^\dagger,(56.0,16),(51.75,16),(56.0,32),(203.15,32),(105.651,32),(416.524,32),(416.524,64)^\ddagger$ |
| Random rungs | 60 @ $L{=}16$, $\beta\in[1,60]$; 12 @ $L{=}32$, $\beta\in[2,60]$; 6 @ $L{=}8$, $\beta\in[1,8]$ (v3\_scale: +24 @ $L{=}32$) | 60 @ $L{=}16$, $\beta\in[4,430]$; 12 @ $L{=}32$, $\beta\in[8,430]$; 6 @ $L{=}8$, $\beta\in[4,60]$; 24 @ $L{=}32$, $\beta\in[8,430]$ |
| Held out (generalization test) | $\beta=20.0$, $L{=}16$ | $\beta=80.0$, $L{=}16$ |
| Deployed ladder base | $\beta=1.347$, $L{=}8$ | $\beta=28.0$, $L{=}16$ ($\dagger$) |
| Ladder schedule | $L=8\to16\to32\to64$ at $\beta=4.0,\,14.15,\,55.02$ | $L=16\to32\to64$ at $\beta=105.651,\,416.524$ |
| Network capacity | hidden 56 / depth 4 (v2); hidden 80 / depth 5 (v3\_scale) | hidden 64 / depth 4 |
| Epochs / batch | 100 / 16 (v2); 80 / 16 (v3\_scale) | 120 / 32 |
| `sigma_min_beta_coef` | 0.30 | 0.35 |
| `topo_weight` | 0.1 | 0.1 |

$\dagger$: u2's $(28.0,16)$ rung doubles as the ladder base.
$\ddagger$: the top u2 rung, $(416.524,64)$, is `train: false` — a held-out
control, used only to validate, never trained on.

**Caveat on the high-$\beta$ random rungs (both studies, up to $\beta=430$ for
u2): topology there is *installed*, not sampled.** Confirmed directly in
`u2_2d/scripts/01_generate_data.py`: every rung above model-$\beta\approx12.9$
sets `seed_exact_sectors: true`. $Q$ is drawn from the closed form, imposed
by `set_topological_charge`, and only *then* does HMC equilibrate *local*
structure at that fixed sector; a `sector_augment` step further manufactures
charged-sector copies since natural $P(|Q|>0)$ is negligible at high
$\beta$. The script's own comment says this is necessary "because no local
dynamics equilibrates $P(Q)$ ... the only cheap global move is even-charge"
— **that comment is stale and must not be quoted in the paper.** It predates
the marginal winding move (`charge_step=1`, §4), which CLAUDE.md's own
measurements show is a working global move for *both* parities — mobile at
every $\beta$ tested at $L=16$ (72522 flips at $\beta=14$, 61403 at
$\beta=28$, correct odd fraction throughout), in exactly the regime the
old even-only proposal died in. The reason data generation still runs only
the free even move (`winding_charge_step=2`, the deployed default) is
simply that `seed_exact_sectors` already installs the correct charge of
*either* parity directly from the closed form before HMC starts, so there
is nothing left for a global odd move to do at that stage — not because
odd is otherwise unreachable. So the training range to $\beta=430$ is real
and deployed, and it is honest only about local structure, which is also
all the network is ever asked to reproduce, since $Q$ (any parity) is
supplied externally by the identical transport mechanism (§4) at generation
time too. State this precisely in the paper: local structure is what is
learned; topology, of either parity, is never learned by the network and
is imposed by an exact mechanism at both training-data-construction time
and generation time.

**The deployed checkpoints do not actually use the random-rung recipe
above.** `det_score_net.pt` (u2) and the u1 checkpoint of record were both
trained on the *fixed* rungs only; the random-rung ("v2") data recipe was
tested as a coverage-widening experiment and, at matched network capacity,
**regressed** lift precision in both studies (the tuned-sweep-count needed
to hit the exact plaquette rose from 5 to 30 sweeps in both) — diagnosed as
capacity dilution, not a defect of random-beta coverage itself (u1's own
recipe uses random beta successfully, at higher capacity and more gradient
steps per epoch). This is why the u1/u2 comparison above is presented
side-by-side rather than as "the same recipe, ported": u2's random-rung
config is a documented negative result, kept as data but not deployed.
